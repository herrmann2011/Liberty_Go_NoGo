How this is calculated. Temperature, humidity, and wind forecasts are sourced from the National Weather Service via api.weather.gov — the same forecast data used by NOAA's website, The Weather Channel, and other major U.S. weather services. Cloud cover, precipitation amounts, and historical conditions are sourced from Open-Meteo's high-resolution forecast. The two sources are merged so that NWS provides the forecaster-adjusted temperature and humidity values, while Open-Meteo provides the cloud-cover and precipitation detail not exposed by NWS's hourly endpoint. Sunrise and sunset times are calculated from Open-Meteo's astronomical endpoint for the exact latitude and longitude.

The Liberty activity score. The decision logic blends six factors, weighted in the order below. (1) Temperature + relative humidity ≥ 100 is treated as a hard minimum. Below that threshold, ambient humidity is insufficient to keep the leaf surface wet long enough for the herbicide to absorb, and control of established weeds drops off sharply. (2) Temperature + relative humidity ≥ 150 is the optimal range, where field performance on larger and more difficult weeds is most consistent. (3) Full sun is essential. Glufosinate is light-activated and requires active photosynthesis to convert to its herbicidal form. Published research (Coetzer et al. 2001, Takano & Dayan 2020, Purdue Weed Science) consistently shows midday applications under full sun outperforming early-morning, late-evening, or overcast applications by 15–40% on hard-to-control species. (4) Wind between 3 and 10 mph is ideal. Below 3 mph raises the risk of temperature-inversion drift; above 10 mph exceeds the label limit for ground application, and above 15 mph is treated as a hard stop. (5) Air temperature between 60 °F and 85 °F is preferred. Above 85 °F with low relative humidity, volatility and drift risk increase materially. (6) Rain within four hours of application is a hard stop, consistent with the Liberty label's rainfast requirement.

The hourly view shades each daylight hour green (full activation conditions), amber (workable but suboptimal), red (label violation or insufficient activity), or dark (outside daylight hours — glufosinate is not active without sunlight). The recommended start time is the earliest green hour after sunrise that leaves sufficient daylight for the activated herbicide to translocate before sunset.

This tool is decision support, not a label. It does not account for tank-mix partners, adjuvant selection, nozzle configuration, sprayer speed, weed size or species, prior herbicide passes, crop stage, or other field-level factors that require operator judgment. The published research it is calibrated against (Coetzer, Takano & Dayan, Purdue, Iowa State, Michigan State Extension) provides a defensible view of the environmental spray window only. Always read and follow the Liberty / Liberty ULTRA label.
